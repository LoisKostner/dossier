About

I work at the intersection of computational fluid dynamics, aero-thermodynamics and turbomachinery. I completed my BSc in Aerospace Engineering at the University of Bologna with a final grade of 110/110, and I begin my MSc at Technische Universität München in October 2026.

My simulation work is built around end-to-end workflows: geometry preparation, meshing, solver setup, verification, and post-processing. What I care about most is the middle step. A result without a mesh-independence study, a near-wall validity check and an honest uncertainty budget is an opinion, not an answer, so each report states plainly what was verified, what was simplified, and where the conclusions stop.

Alongside that, I co-founded Aurora Rocketry and spent eighteen months turning it from a plan on paper into a hundred-member association that made its debut at the European Rocketry Challenge. That side of the work (statutes, budgets, funding applications, recruitment) taught me as much as the simulations did.

Both of my completed projects are studies of Nemesis, the sounding rocket developed at Aurora Rocketry, so the aerodynamics, the meshing practice and the verification discipline behind them come from a single vehicle. From here the direction changes. I intend to specialise in aero engines and turbomachinery, carrying the numerical and aerodynamic methods I already have into a field that demands a good deal more. The NASA C3X vane study is the first step that way.

Sep 2026– ongoing In progress

Aerothermal CFD Analysis of the NASA C3X Turbine Vane

The NASA C3X vane is a reference validation case for turbine aero-thermal prediction: measured in detail and published openly. The objective is to predict the external heat-transfer distribution along the vane surface and compare it against those measurements. I reconstructed the vane profile from the NASA CR-174827 dataset and prepared a periodic 2.5D domain consistent with the experimental cascade; the current work is a fully structured multi-block mesh in ANSYS ICEM CFD, refined near the wall to resolve the thermal boundary layer rather than model it, with the RANS chain in ANSYS Fluent being set up alongside.

ANSYS ICEM CFD / Structured multi-block meshing / ANSYS Fluent / RANS / External heat transfer / Turbomachinery

Oct 2025– Mar 2026 BSc thesis

CFD Analysis of the Propulsive Jet Effect on the Aerodynamic Coefficients of a Sounding Rocket

In common CFD practice the exhaust jet is left out of a rocket model and the nozzle exit is capped with a closed surface. This study measures what that simplification costs. The jet and no-jet configurations of the Nemesis sounding rocket were run through one identical workflow, using a compressible density-based solver with k-ω SST and wall functions, then compared.

Domain- and mesh-independence studies, stationarity and y⁺ checks back every reported difference with an uncertainty budget. Lift and pitching moment are essentially unaffected. Drag is not, and the difference traces almost entirely to the base region under the overexpanded plume.

ANSYS Fluent / Fluent Meshing · poly-hexcore / Density-based solver / RANS k-ω SST / Supersonic · overexpanded jet / Mesh & domain independence / Uncertainty quantification / MATLAB

Jul 2025– Oct 2025 EuRoC report

CFD-FEM Aero-Structural Analysis of a Sounding Rocket

Structural clearance of Nemesis under the worst combination of loads it could meet in flight: the atmospheric state at maximum dynamic pressure, the maximum angle of attack from the trajectory, and peak motor thrust, all superimposed.

I developed the CFD-to-FEM load-transfer workflow adopted in the team's EuRoC Technical Report. Aerodynamic loads computed in ANSYS Fluent were integrated over a surface partition matching the structural breakdown, then applied to an equivalent beam model where inertia relief reconstructs the internal load path. Those internal actions fed local checks on every component on the critical path, from bulkhead to electronics-bay rods.

ANSYS Fluent / ANSYS Static Structural / Equivalent beam model / Inertia relief / CFD → FEM load transfer / Safety-margin assessment / SolidWorks

Co-founder & Vice-President

Aurora Rocketry · University of Bologna, Forlì (IT)

Aurora Rocketry is the student rocketry association of the University of Bologna. I founded it in March 2024 together with its first President, starting from a design study and a single fixed objective: reaching the European Rocketry Challenge. Eighteen months later the team counted more than a hundred students across eight departments and made its competition debut in Constância (PT) with the sounding rocket Nemesis.

I owned the side of the project that has to work before any engineering can happen: legal existence, money, people and representation. On Nemesis I also contributed the aero-structural analysis and structural verification that went into the EuRoC Technical Report.

  • Facilities & permits. Negotiated dedicated workbench space at the MaSTeR Lab and opened the NOTAM launch-authorisation process with ENAV contacts.
  • Recruitment. Ran the second recruitment campaign across the Forlì and Cesena campuses, introducing a real selection process; designed the recruitment flyers.
  • EuRoC 2025. Wrote the motivation letter for the team's application, co-produced the official presentation video, and organised travel, logistics and material transport to Portugal.
  • Representation. Represented the association at IRESS 2025, the 6th CASSINI Hackathon and the We Make Future 2025 fair, designing the exhibition roll-ups for the latter; also designed the association's website.
